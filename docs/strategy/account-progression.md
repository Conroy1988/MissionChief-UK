# Cross-Service Account Progression

A strong MissionChief UK account expands mission generation only when the current network can absorb the additional vehicles, personnel, patients, prisoners, towing, command and travel-time pressure. Station count alone does not prove readiness.

!!! info "Evidence boundary"
    Service relationships and resource semantics link to the completed canonical service guides. Account stages, fleet posture, reserve floors and recommended service order are strategy models, not official unlock rules. Costs, capacities and thresholds remain unknown wherever the verified records do not publish them.

**Current evidence baseline:** 28 July 2026.

## The universal decision loop

```text
Observe current mission pressure
              ↓
Identify the narrowest bottleneck
              ↓
Correct quantity, geography, training or infrastructure
              ↓
Test simultaneous demand with protected reserve
              ↓
Restore the network to Ready
              ↓
Activate one controlled expansion
              ↓
Observe the changed mission mix
```

Do not add several generators at once when one controlled activation can reveal the real constraint.

## Eight universal expansion gates

| Gate | Question | Recommended pass condition |
|---|---|---|
| Routine reserve | Can ordinary Fire, Police and Ambulance demand continue after a normal dispatch? | Useful staffed frontline capacity remains in each primary response cluster |
| Personnel | Are the required qualifications and mission personnel actually available? | Vehicles and trained cohorts can dispatch simultaneously without hidden sharing |
| Geography | Will the new resource reach the missions, destinations or access points it is intended to serve? | Real route coverage improves rather than only filling a visual map gap |
| Cross-service chain | Are partner services and handoffs ready? | No mission family depends on an uncommissioned Police, Ambulance, Fire, HART, SAR, maritime or railway capability |
| Command and support | Can independent command, welfare, mass-casualty and specialist-support rows be met? | Each published group has its own valid resource allocation |
| Throughput | Can patients, prisoners, recovery assets and returning vehicles complete the full cycle? | Transport and return time do not silently exhaust the fleet |
| Concurrency | Can two representative incidents operate together? | No vehicle, trailer, carrier, crew or specialist role is counted twice |
| Recovery | Can the network return to useful geography after the incidents clear? | The selected stress scenario can be run again with protected reserve |

When one gate fails, correct it before expanding. A new building rarely fixes a personnel, destination or geographic bottleneck by itself.

## Recommended readiness states

These are planning labels, not MissionChief statuses.

| State | Recommended meaning | Expansion posture |
|---|---|---|
| **Ready** | Current representative missions can dispatch while protected routine and specialist reserve remains | One controlled expansion may proceed |
| **Watch** | Reserve exists, but one capability or region is approaching its floor | Observe and correct before further specialist activation |
| **Degraded** | Current missions can finish, but a critical service, specialist or response zone has no independent reserve | Stop expansion and rebuild the depleted chain |
| **Recovery** | Incidents are clearing, but vehicles, crews, patients, prisoners or towing assets have not returned to useful positions | Do not treat completion as restored readiness |
| **Unavailable** | Another reference incident requires alliance assistance or leaves essential calls unsupported | Restore local core capacity before adding generators |

## Stage 1 — Tri-service foundation

Build the first operational network around routine Fire, Police and Ambulance demand.

### Objectives

- staffed frontline Fire capacity with useful reserve;
- routine Police Cars with patrol and custody headroom;
- Ambulances positioned for treatment, transport and hospital return;
- practical station geography rather than equal visual spacing;
- enough funds and personnel to recover from ordinary overlap.

### Recommended posture

| Service | Foundation capability |
|---|---|
| Fire and Rescue | Frontline pumping, one useful reserve route and emerging Rescue Support/command awareness |
| Police | Routine patrol capacity, prisoner-handling awareness and protected local response |
| Ambulance | Treatment/transport capacity sized from patient cycle time rather than station count |

### Do not progress when

- one routine incident empties the local Fire fleet;
- Ambulances remain away at hospitals while new patients queue;
- Police custody journeys remove every nearby unit;
- the next station is selected only because the map looks empty;
- personnel are assumed rather than assigned.

## Stage 2 — First specialist capability

Choose the first specialist from observed mission pressure rather than a universal purchase list.

### Common first-pressure families

| Pressure | Likely planning response | Evidence check |
|---|---|---|
| Repeated rescue-support delays | Commission Rescue Support or verified dual-capability response | Mission Lookup and Fire guide |
| Command groups appearing | Train and distribute the relevant Fire, Ambulance or service command route | Preserve independent alternative groups |
| Repeated aerial demand | Add the verified aerial capability within realistic travel time | Do not invent missing economics |
| Armed, roads or dog demand | Commission the relevant Police vehicle and education | Keep routine patrol reserve separate |
| HART demand | Build the full PRV/SRV/training/command chain | Do not buy uncrewed specialist vehicles |
| Coastal or mountain demand | Commission the complete access, towing, vessel or off-road system | Preserve exact resource classes |

### Specialist commissioning rule

```text
Verified mission pressure
          ↓
Correct infrastructure
          ↓
Vehicle / trailer / vessel / equipment
          ↓
Qualified personnel
          ↓
Carrier / towing / launch path
          ↓
Geographic placement
          ↓
Replacement depth
```

Owning only one link in this chain does not create operational capability.

## Stage 3 — Stable regional clusters

Move from individual stations to independently functional response clusters.

### A response cluster should contain

- routine Fire, Police and Ambulance capacity;
- one protected local command route;
- access to common technical-rescue and specialist support;
- realistic hospital and custody destinations;
- documented reinforcement routes from neighbouring clusters;
- enough trained personnel to avoid implicit double assignment.

### Cluster test

1. Select one representative Fire mission.
2. Add one patient or Police-heavy mission.
3. Dispatch the guaranteed requirements in Fleet Planner.
4. Allocate every alternative group independently.
5. Keep a documented local response floor outside the incidents.
6. Confirm patient, custody and return-time pressure.
7. Mark the cluster Ready only when the resources return to useful positions.

## Stage 4 — Service specialisation

Expand into the service whose complete response chain is already closest to readiness.

### Service progression matrix

| Programme | Activate when | Principal hidden bottlenecks |
|---|---|---|
| Fire specialists | Frontline and command reserve survives extension-driven incidents | Training, carrier/pod availability, water/foam distinction |
| Ambulance and HART | Routine transport remains resilient during specialist dispatch | Patient throughput, command, welfare, SORT/HART cohorts |
| Police and Public Safety | Patrol, supervision and custody remain available | Educated officer cohorts, prisoner capacity, specialist geography |
| Coastguard and Lifeboat | Shore, vessel, towing and trained crew chains are complete | Launch access, tow vehicles, vessel class, patient handoff |
| Mountain Rescue / SAR | Off-road, command, dog, aerial and personnel chains are ready | Alternative groups, remote travel, active-Drone semantics |
| Bomb Disposal | Crew/equipment and partner services are ready before HQ growth | Command/heavy/diver resources, active Drone, HART/Police/Coastguard |
| Airfield Operations | ARFF, operations, water/foam/HazMat and medical systems are complete | Training, independent command groups, mass-casualty pressure |
| Recovery / HGV | Towing outcomes can be cleared without inventing response rows | Asset class, corridor geography, towing-return time |
| Railway | Railway Police, rail access and cross-service specialists are commissioned | EIU, Road Rail, BA/Foam, personnel, command and tunnel geography |

## Stage 5 — Cross-service integration

At this stage, expansion decisions should be based on shared dependencies rather than individual service pages.

## Cross-service dependency matrix

| Mission family | Primary systems | Frequent partner systems |
|---|---|---|
| Major urban Fire | Fire, command, technical rescue | Ambulance/HART, Police, mass casualty, welfare |
| Public order | Police personnel, supervision and transport | Ambulance, custody, dogs, mounted response |
| Airfield | ARFF, operations, foam, water and command | HART, mass casualty, Police, HazMat, hospitals |
| Railway | Railway Police, EIU, Road Rail and Fire support | HART, Ambulance command, SAR, dogs, HazMat |
| Flood / inland water | Flood Rescue, pumping and water access | Fire, Coastguard/Lifeboat, Police, HART |
| Maritime | Shore, vessel, towing and air rescue | Ambulance, Police, Fire, Bomb Disposal |
| Mountain / SAR | Off-road, search command, dogs and aerial search | HART, Fire, Police, Coastguard helicopter |
| Bomb Disposal | Crew/equipment, command and infrastructure | Fire, Police, HART, Coastguard/Lifeboat, Railway |
| Recovery | Car/truck towing outcome | Fire, Police, Ambulance, Roads Policing |

### Integration rule

Do not activate a mission family because the primary service alone is ready. Activate it when every hard partner dependency can operate with reserve.

## Stage 6 — Major-incident resilience

Prepare the account for overlapping high-load missions rather than one record at a time.

### Required operating layers

1. **Incident response** — every guaranteed, alternative, personnel and patient field can be supported.
2. **Protected local reserve** — routine missions still dispatch in exposed regions.
3. **Strategic specialist reserve** — command, aerial, welfare, mass-casualty, towing and access resources are not single points of failure.
4. **Regional reinforcement** — neighbouring clusters support the incident without emptying themselves.
5. **Alliance contingency** — exceptional peaks can request support without hiding permanent local gaps.
6. **Recovery** — the network returns to useful geography before more generators activate.

### Reference stress test

Choose two incidents from different mission families, for example:

- major Fire plus public order;
- airfield plus railway;
- flood plus remote search;
- Bomb Disposal harbour plus ordinary urban demand;
- patient-heavy major incident plus HGV recovery.

Use verified guaranteed rows, preserve every alternative group, and leave patient/transport conversion unknown unless published.

## Stage 7 — Mature regional network

A mature account is not defined by a station total. It is a network that can:

- operate several response clusters independently;
- absorb specialist and major incidents without regional collapse;
- sustain trained replacement cohorts;
- maintain patient, custody, towing and launch throughput;
- distribute rare resources by travel time;
- use alliance support as contingency rather than routine dependency;
- recover deterministically after large incidents;
- add new game content through the evidence workflow without inventing values.

### Mature operating cadence

| Cadence | Review |
|---|---|
| Per expansion | Readiness gates, exact mission pressure and funding recovery |
| Weekly or periodic | Unmet requirements, long travel, patient/custody queues and specialist reuse |
| After major incident | Reserve erosion and recovery-to-readiness |
| After game update | Mission, vehicle, training and infrastructure evidence drift |
| Before new service family | Complete cross-service commissioning test |

## Account archetypes

### Dense urban account

- Shorter travel permits some shared reserve.
- Hospital and custody throughput can dominate despite close stations.
- Distribute rare specialists to avoid congestion and simultaneous-demand failures.
- Use several smaller response clusters rather than one giant central fleet.

### Rural or dispersed account

- Local reserve matters more because neighbours may be distant.
- Water, air, off-road, command and patient transport can require earlier geographic duplication.
- Station placement should follow real road corridors and destinations.
- Alliance support may cover rare peaks but should not replace local core response.

### Coastal and waterway account

- Shore response, launch points, vessel class and towing form the placement model.
- Flood, Coastguard, Lifeboat, Fire and Ambulance capability overlap.
- Preserve coastal and inland-water systems separately.
- Include patients, prisoners and return-to-launch time.

### Alliance-supported account

- Build enough local routine capacity to contribute safely.
- Record which rare capabilities are realistically available from allies.
- Avoid several members relying on the same single alliance specialist.
- Agree request, dispatch and release etiquette before major incidents.

## Expansion stop conditions

Pause expansion when any of these is true:

- the account is Degraded or in Recovery;
- a new extension would generate missions without the complete response chain;
- one trained cohort is counted across several vehicles;
- patient or custody throughput is persistently growing;
- a rare command, welfare, aerial, towing or access resource has no replacement;
- the next station does not improve practical route coverage;
- alliance support is the only solution for routine incidents;
- missing evidence is being treated as zero or as an assumed game rule.

## Recovery-to-readiness

After any high-load period:

1. list vehicles, aircraft, vessels, trailers, containers and crews still committed;
2. restore routine Fire, Police and Ambulance cover in exposed clusters;
3. restore command and hard-blocking specialists;
4. rebuild patient, custody, towing and destination throughput;
5. return rare resources to their intended geography;
6. restore trained replacement cohorts;
7. rerun the selected stress scenario;
8. correct recurring deficits before another expansion.

## Account progression checklist

- [ ] routine Fire, Police and Ambulance reserve is protected;
- [ ] station placement follows route demand and destinations;
- [ ] specialists are commissioned as complete vehicle/personnel/logistics chains;
- [ ] available and required personnel semantics remain separate;
- [ ] alternatives, probabilities and conditions remain distinct;
- [ ] patient, prisoner and recovery outcomes are not converted into invented requirements;
- [ ] every active mission family has its cross-service partners ready;
- [ ] two representative incidents have been tested together;
- [ ] alliance support is treated as contingency;
- [ ] the network is Ready before another generator activates;
- [ ] unpublished values remain unknown rather than zero.
