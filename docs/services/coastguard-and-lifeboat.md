# Coastguard and Lifeboat Operational Progression

Coastguard and Lifeboat operations combine shore response, command, specialist rescue, vessels, aircraft, trailers, towing vehicles, launch geography and cross-service patient handling. The safe planning unit is therefore the complete response chain rather than an isolated vehicle or boat.

!!! info "Evidence boundary"
    Verified statements reproduce current canonical mission, vehicle, infrastructure, personnel and training records. Fleet sizes, reserve floors, placement patterns and commissioning sequences are recommendations. Unpublished station economics, capacities, course durations, launch behaviour and unlock thresholds remain unknown.

**Current evidence baseline:** 28 July 2026.

## Command doctrine

Use this order when expanding:

1. **Shore response first** — maintain CRV and common-support coverage before adding rare maritime specialists.
2. **Vessel class by mission evidence** — ILB, ALB, rescue watercraft, inland boat and hovercraft are not interchangeable.
3. **Trailer and towing chain together** — a stored trailer without a compatible staffed tow vehicle is not dispatch-ready.
4. **Training before specialist activation** — command, mud, rope, flood, lifeboat and hovercraft capability requires the published education where known.
5. **Geography before duplication** — place stations and launch systems against usable road, coast, estuary, harbour, river, lake and ocean access.
6. **Cross-service reserve before generator growth** — coastal missions may add Fire, Ambulance, HART, Police, SAR and patient-transport pressure.

## Maritime response chain

```text
Coastal, flood or ocean mission pressure
                ↓
Shore response and command
                ↓
Specialist mud / rope / flood capability
                ↓
Correct vessel, aircraft or trailer class
                ↓
Compatible towing and trained personnel
                ↓
Patient / prisoner handling and cross-service support
                ↓
Return to launch-ready regional reserve
```

A resource may be at the correct station but still unavailable because its crew, trailer, tow vehicle, launch access or partner service is committed elsewhere.

### ILB and ALB planning

- Use Mission Lookup to preserve exact class requirements.
- Position by navigable coverage and route-to-water access, not straight-line map distance alone.
- Maintain trained reserve before treating one vessel as a regional dependency.
- Include patients and prisoners in capacity planning where the mission publishes them.
- Do not treat vessel transport capacity as a replacement for Ambulance or Police requirements unless the mission contract supports the handoff.

## Coastguard air rescue

The verified **Coastguard Rescue Helicopter** provides:

- air and ocean rescue;
- patient treatment and transport;
- Coastal Air Rescue Operations training;
- SAR Airbase operation;
- fixed crew of 4;
- capacity for 16 patients;
- verified cost of **300,000 credits** or **30 coins**.

A representative mission may require the aircraft directly or probabilistically. Preserve the mission field rather than dispatching it as guaranteed on every coastal incident.

### Air-rescue placement

- Position by regional travel advantage and offshore reach.
- Maintain ground and vessel capacity because air rescue does not replace scene, launch or towing requirements.
- Include destination and return time in readiness.
- Train replacement personnel before treating one aircraft as resilient.
- Preserve probabilistic requirements as probabilities.

### Activation gates

Before activating another specialist generator, confirm:

1. the exact mission family is relevant to the account;
2. the correct specialist vehicle or trailer exists;
3. a compatible staffed towing path exists;
4. the required published training is complete;
5. shore command and common support remain available;
6. patient and cross-service demand can be absorbed;
7. another mission can be handled without borrowing the same vessel, aircraft, tow vehicle or crew.

## Personnel-state planning

Verified coastal missions include roles such as **Mud Rescue Operator**. Mission data distinguishes personnel:

- required at the incident;
- available before mission generation;
- ranged, average-minimum or probabilistic where published.

Do not flatten these states into one universal staffing number. Vehicle crew ranges and mission personnel roles are different contracts.

## Seven commissioning gates

| Gate | Question | Recommended pass condition |
|---|---|---|
| Shore reserve | Can CRV and command demand be met without emptying the coast? | Useful shore response remains in each active zone |
| Correct class | Does the mission need shore, ILB, ALB, air, flood, mud, rope or another specific capability? | No generic watercraft substitution is assumed |
| Towing | Are the trailer and compatible tow vehicle simultaneously available? | One complete staffed logistics chain can dispatch |
| Training | Is the verified education complete? | Vehicle, crew and course relationship are aligned |
| Geography | Can the resource reach a usable launch or incident area? | Actual road/water/air coverage is practical |
| Cross-service | Can patients, Police, Fire, HART or SAR dependencies be supported? | The complete mission chain is ready |
| Recovery | Can the system relaunch after the first incident? | Vehicles, trailers and crews return to useful positions |

## Geographic doctrine

### Coastal station network

- Position CRVs, command and support against road access to beaches, harbours, marinas and cliffs.
- Distribute specialist units so one remote mission does not strip the whole coast.
- Place tow vehicles with their intended trailers.
- Use actual road-to-launch and water coverage rather than map distance alone.

### Inland-water network

- Place Lifeboat 4x4 and Inland Rescue Boat near River and Lake access.
- Preserve Fire command and Ambulance support where inland missions publish them.
- Avoid assuming ocean vessels replace trailered inland capability.
- Duplicate towing when one vehicle serves several trailers.

### Flood network

- Align Flood Rescue trailers with CRVs, compatible tow vehicles and Fire flood resources.
- Distribute by river, canal, low-lying road and coastal corridor.
- Include severe-weather concurrency across several regions.
- Maintain routine Coastguard cover outside the flood incident.

### Ocean network

- Place ILB, ALB and rescue helicopter by navigable reach and mission class.
- Protect replacement crews and vessel reserve.
- Account for patients generated at mission end.
- Include return and re-launch time before declaring readiness restored.

## Recovery-to-readiness

After a maritime or specialist incident:

1. record shore vehicles, vessels, aircraft, trailers and crews still committed;
2. restore CRV and command cover in exposed areas;
3. return tow vehicles and trailers to compatible locations;
4. verify trained personnel remain assigned to the intended resources;
5. restore ILB, ALB and air-rescue regional coverage;
6. review patients, transports and cross-service missions still active;
7. rerun the selected concurrency scenario;
8. correct recurring geography, towing or training bottlenecks before more expansion.

Mission completion is not proof that a vessel is relaunched, a trailer is paired or a region is ready.

## Common failures

| Failure | Operational symptom | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Treating every boat as interchangeable | The wrong vessel class attends while the mission remains open | Preserve exact mission resources |
| Owning trailers without tow reserve | Specialist equipment cannot dispatch | Keep compatible staffed towing paths |
| Centralising shore specialists | One incident empties a wide coast | Distribute by travel-time zone |
| Activating extensions before training | New specialist missions generate before crews are ready | Complete commissioning and replacement cohorts |
| Ignoring exclusive towing | Mud Decontamination trailers remain stored | Preserve the Mud Rescue Unit towing dependency |
| Counting crew seats as mission personnel | Vehicles are staffed but Mud Rescue Operator requirements remain open | Audit personnel roles separately |
| Ignoring launch geography | Nearby map resources have poor real access | Plan road-to-water and navigable coverage |
| Treating patient capacity as Ambulance replacement | Patients cannot complete the intended handoff | Preserve mission and transport contracts |
| Planning Coastguard alone | Fire, Police, HART, SAR or Ambulance rows remain open | Audit the entire mission chain |
| Treating completion as restored readiness | Vessels and trailers remain displaced | Run the recovery sequence |

## Operational readiness checklist

- [ ] shore-response and command reserve survives a normal dispatch;
- [ ] ILB, ALB, aircraft and trailer classes remain separate;
- [ ] every trailer has a compatible staffed towing vehicle;
- [ ] published training names are preserved and unknown durations remain unknown;
- [ ] Flood, Mud, Rope, Hovercraft and Beach Lifeguard generators are activated only after readiness;
- [ ] vehicle crew and mission personnel states are not conflated;
- [ ] patient and prisoner capacities are included without inventing substitution;
- [ ] road, launch, water and air geography has been tested;
- [ ] cross-service dependencies are ready;
- [ ] two reference incidents have been tested together;
- [ ] alliance support is contingency;
- [ ] recovery restores useful regional positions;
- [ ] unpublished values remain unknown rather than zero.
