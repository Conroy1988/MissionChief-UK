# Fire and Rescue Service

The Fire and Rescue Service is the broadest operational expansion route in MissionChief UK. A strong fire estate is not simply a large collection of appliances: it is a network with enough frontline capacity for routine demand, enough reserve to absorb simultaneous incidents, and specialist capability distributed where it can arrive in time.

!!! info "How to read this guide"
    **Verified** statements reproduce current repository evidence. **Community-reported** values are retained with their lower evidence status. **Recommended** fleet sizes and progression gates are strategic planning guidance, not hidden game rules or guaranteed unlock thresholds.

**Current evidence baseline:** 27 July 2026. The guide links to the complete canonical mission catalogue and the 100%-resolved resource decision estate, but unpublished game values remain unknown.

## Command doctrine

Use this order when expanding:

1. **Frontline capacity first** — routine incidents should not consume every available pumping appliance.
2. **Geographic coverage second** — a specialist that is always too far away is not operationally available.
3. **Personnel before hardware** — buy or activate trained capability only when its crew can be sustained.
4. **Specialists in response to evidence** — add capability because your mission mix requires it, not because a vehicle exists in the market.
5. **Resilience before another service** — expansion is safer when the current fire network can survive overlapping calls without relying on the alliance for every reserve move.

## Five expansion gates

Treat a new station, extension or specialist purchase as ready only when all relevant gates are satisfied.

| Gate | Question | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|
| Frontline reserve | Can routine missions be dispatched without emptying the area? | At least one useful frontline appliance remains available across the local response cluster after a normal dispatch. |
| Geography | Will the new unit reach the incidents it is intended to cover? | Placement improves actual road response coverage rather than only filling a visual map gap. |
| Personnel | Can the vehicle be crewed today and after simultaneous dispatches? | The required trained personnel are assigned locally with operational reserve. |
| Evidence | Is the capability appearing in your current mission pressure? | Mission Lookup or recent dispatch history shows a repeated requirement, probability or prerequisite. |
| Recovery | Can the account absorb the purchase without stopping essential growth? | The purchase does not consume the funds needed for frontline replacement, staffing or the next planned station. |

!!! tip "Recommended decision rule"
    When only one gate fails, correct that deficit before expanding. Buying a specialist vehicle rarely fixes a shortage of pumps, personnel or coverage.

## Frontline pumping capacity

Frontline appliances create the operational base from which every specialist fleet works. The exact appliance chosen may provide additional capability, but the planning question remains the same: **how many independent incidents can the network support before specialist availability collapses?**

### Recommended reserve model

Use response clusters rather than treating each station in isolation.

- Keep **one reserve frontline appliance for every three to four appliances routinely committed at the same time**.
- In a dense urban area, neighbouring stations may share that reserve when travel times are short.
- In rural or separated areas, keep the reserve physically closer to the demand because neighbouring coverage may be too slow.
- Do not count an appliance as reserve when its crew is normally borrowed by a specialist vehicle.

This is a planning ratio, not a MissionChief requirement.

## Specialist capability order

There is no single compulsory purchase order for every account. The following sequence is a **recommended risk order** for a general-purpose fire network.

### 1. Rescue-support coverage

Secure rescue-support capability without removing too much frontline pumping capacity. A dual-capability appliance may help where its evidence and current market details are acceptable, while a separate specialist unit may provide clearer availability in a larger network.

### 2. Command coverage

Introduce Fire Officer capability when command requirements become regular enough that alliance borrowing or long-distance response delays incidents. Spread command units across response clusters as the account grows.

### 3. Aerial coverage

Add aerial capability once the current mission mix produces verified aerial requirements or probabilities. A single central unit can cover an early compact network; a wider network usually benefits more from geographic duplication than from stacking multiple aerials at one station.

### 4. Water and foam capability

Treat water supply and foam as different operational needs. The Water Ladder with CAFS is a verified foam-capable appliance with published cost, staffing and Fire Support compatibility. Water Carrier capability is separately verified for bulk-water demand. Review the mission rather than assuming one replaces the other.

### 5. HazMat and technical rescue

HazMat requires trained personnel, so the three-day course is part of the purchase decision. Technical Rescue Extensions are verified as countable mission-generation preconditions, while compatible buildings, price and construction details remain unpublished in the current canonical record. Activating mission generation before response capability is ready can create avoidable pressure.

## Recommended fleet templates

These templates describe **capability slots**, not mandatory game requirements. Adjust them for geography, mission generation, alliance support and the actual vehicles available to the account.

### Foundation network

Suitable for a compact early fire estate.

| Capability | Recommended position |
|---|---|
| Frontline pumping | 4–6 appliances across the local coverage area |
| Operational reserve | 1 frontline appliance not committed by a normal dispatch |
| Rescue support | 1 available capability when repeated mission demand appears |
| Command | Alliance-backed initially, then 1 local Fire Officer when delays become regular |
| Aerial | 1 centrally placed unit after verified demand appears |
| Foam, water and HazMat | Add only against demonstrated mission pressure and personnel readiness |

### Developing network

Suitable when several incidents are commonly active together.

| Capability | Recommended position |
|---|---|
| Frontline pumping | 8–12 appliances across two or more response clusters |
| Operational reserve | At least 2 useful frontline appliances across the network |
| Rescue support | 1 per main operating cluster or enough dual-capability coverage to avoid a single point of failure |
| Command | 1–2 Fire Officers distributed geographically |
| Aerial | 1 per broad coverage zone where one unit no longer reaches demand reliably |
| Foam and water | At least one available capability of each type when the mission mix regularly calls for them |
| HazMat | One trained response group, with replacement personnel planned before duplication |

### Established network

Suitable for a wide account handling sustained concurrency.

| Capability | Recommended position |
|---|---|
| Frontline pumping | Size by measured concurrent demand, with reserve maintained in every major cluster |
| Command and aerial | Duplicate by travel-time zone rather than station count alone |
| Rescue support | No single unit should be the only response for the entire account |
| Water, foam and HazMat | Distributed according to mission geography and extension-driven generation |
| Containers and carriers | Planned as a logistics system: carrier availability must be assessed separately from container ownership |
| Personnel reserve | Trained crews available for simultaneous incidents, leave, reassignment and future vehicles |

## Urban and rural planning

=== "Urban or dense network"

    - Shorter travel times allow neighbouring stations to share some reserve.
    - Specialist duplication can follow incident concurrency rather than distance alone.
    - Avoid over-centralising: traffic and simultaneous missions can still make a central specialist unavailable.
    - Smaller response clusters should remain independently functional during large incidents.

=== "Rural or dispersed network"

    - Distance makes geographic duplication more important.
    - Keep frontline reserve local rather than relying on a distant station.
    - Water supply, technical rescue and specialist response may need forward placement before incident frequency alone appears to justify it.
    - Use actual route times when comparing station sites.

=== "Alliance-supported account"

    - Alliance support can cover rare specialists during early growth.
    - Do not treat voluntary alliance response as guaranteed reserve.
    - Build local capability when repeated borrowing delays completion or prevents independent play.
    - Keep enough frontline capacity to contribute without leaving your own area empty.

## Use the intelligence tools before buying

### Mission Lookup

Use [Mission Lookup](../tools/mission-lookup.md) to inspect current requirements, alternatives, probabilities, patients, personnel and prerequisites. Search the missions actually appearing in your area rather than buying from a generic list.

Use the [Concurrent Fleet Planner](../tools/fleet-planner.md) to multiply guaranteed requirements across several incidents. This exposes whether the shortage is frontline volume, a specialist single point of failure, or trained personnel.

## Common expansion failures

| Failure | Operational symptom | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Buying specialists before pumps | Specialist vehicles remain available while routine missions wait for fire engines | Restore frontline and reserve capacity first |
| Activating extensions too early | New mission families appear before the response fleet or trained personnel are ready | Delay activation until the full capability chain is staffed |
| Centralising every specialist | Long travel times and one large incident remove capability from the whole account | Duplicate by response zone |
| Counting parked vehicles instead of staffed vehicles | The fleet looks complete but cannot dispatch concurrently | Audit trained personnel and crew assignment |
| Treating probability as guarantee | Too many units are permanently tied up covering occasional requirements | Use the mission probability and concurrency tools |
| Treating unknown as zero | Missing cost, crew or restriction data becomes an unsafe assumption | Keep the field unknown until a reproducible source is added |

## Operational readiness checklist

Before the next fire expansion, confirm:

- [ ] normal dispatches leave useful frontline reserve;
- [ ] the proposed station or specialist improves actual route coverage;
- [ ] required training is complete before operational activation;
- [ ] mission demand is verified through Mission Lookup or account history;
- [ ] extensions will not generate missions the fleet cannot support;
- [ ] specialist resources are not concentrated into one failure point;
- [ ] carriers, containers and crews are planned as separate resources;
- [ ] alliance assistance is treated as support, not guaranteed local capacity;
- [ ] unpublished values remain explicitly unknown;
- [ ] the purchase still leaves funds for staffing and the next essential frontline step.
