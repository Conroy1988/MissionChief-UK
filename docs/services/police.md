# Police and Public Safety Operational Progression

A resilient Police network must preserve routine patrol and custody capacity while building independently deployable armed, roads-policing, dog, public-order, mounted, air-support and railway-response systems. Vehicle ownership alone is insufficient when specialist education, personnel roles, prisoner transport and cross-service dependencies control mission completion.

!!! info "Evidence boundary"
    Verified statements reproduce current canonical mission, vehicle, infrastructure, personnel and training records. Fleet sizes, reserve floors, placement patterns and commissioning sequences are recommendations. Unpublished prices, staffing limits, custody rules, course details and unlock thresholds remain unknown.

**Current evidence baseline:** 28 July 2026.

## Command doctrine

Use this order when expanding:

1. **Routine patrol first** — ordinary Police missions must not consume every dispatchable Police Car.
2. **Custody as a separate chain** — prisoner demand, vehicle capacity and return-to-service time are not identical to scene response.
3. **Training before specialist activation** — armed, roads, dog, mounted and aviation capability depends on the relevant educated personnel.
4. **Public-order personnel before generator growth** — extensions can create missions whose officer and command requirements exceed the visible vehicle count.
5. **Geographic resilience before central concentration** — distribute common and rare resources by real travel-time zone.
6. **Cross-service readiness before major incidents** — Police completion may depend on Ambulance, HART, Fire, Railway or SAR capability.

## Police capacity chain

```text
Mission and prisoner pressure
          ↓
Routine Police Car response
          ↓
Specialist vehicle and educated crew
          ↓
Sergeant / Inspector / specialist personnel
          ↓
Custody or detention transport
          ↓
Cross-service support
          ↓
Vehicles and personnel return to useful geography
```

A response can appear numerically large while still failing because the required educated personnel, prisoner capacity, specialist unit or command role is missing.

## Police and Public Safety Extension

The verified **Police & Public Safety Extension**:

- costs **100,000 credits** or **15 coins**;
- can be built at a Police Station or Large Police Depot;
- enables public-order mission generation;
- enables specialist Police and Public Safety vehicle purchases;
- provides Detention Van parking.

!!! warning "Generator and response are different"
    Extension activation proves that related missions may generate. It does not prove that officer education, supervision, mounted capability, dogs, custody transport or medical support is ready.

### Recommended activation gates

Before activating another extension, confirm:

1. enough routine Police Cars remain for ordinary demand;
2. Level 1 and Level 2 Public Order Officers are available in the relevant region;
3. Police Sergeant, Inspector and Medic requirements can be supported;
4. PSU and mounted transport capacity is staffed;
5. prisoner handling and detention transport are ready;
6. two simultaneous public-order incidents have been tested;
7. alliance support is contingency rather than the only viable response.

### Personnel transport is not personnel availability

A PSU Carrier may transport educated officers, but vehicle capacity does not create qualified personnel. Maintain separate inventories for:

- vehicle seats;
- available Level 1 Public Order Officers;
- available Level 2 Public Order Officers;
- required Sergeants and Inspectors;
- Police Medics;
- reserve personnel for another incident.

### Custody transport

The Detention Van publishes a four-prisoner capacity and dedicated parking rule. Use that verified contract when planning prisoner surges.

Do not assume:

- every prisoner requires a Detention Van;
- every Police vehicle has the same prisoner capacity;
- scene resources become immediately available after custody transport;
- extension parking behaves like an ordinary vehicle slot.

Those behaviours require separate direct evidence.

## Armed-policing planning

### Capability chain

```text
Armed mission pressure
       ↓
ARV, M-RAV or other verified armed response
       ↓
Firearms-trained personnel
       ↓
Routine Police Car and scene-control support
       ↓
Dog / helicopter / HART support where published
       ↓
Custody and recovery reserve
```

### Representative major incident

**Firearms Attack — Major Incident** verifies:

- 12 Police Cars;
- 4 Dog Support Units;
- 1 Police Helicopter;
- 2 PRVs and 2 SRVs;
- 1 Welfare Vehicle;
- 8–15 patients with 100% critical-care probability;
- up to 2 prisoners;
- one required Operational Team Leader.

The official record also retains an armed-personnel field in metadata. Do not convert that field into an unsupported vehicle count.

### Recommended armed posture

- Keep routine Police Cars separate from armed-resource reserve.
- Train a commissioning crew before counting an ARV or M-RAV as operational.
- Add a replacement firearms cohort before geographic duplication.
- Treat Armed Traffic Cars as dual-trained resources rather than free substitutes for every ARV or Traffic Car requirement.
- Test dog, air support, HART and prisoner dependencies with the armed fleet.

## Roads-policing planning

Traffic Cars require the verified three-day Roads Policing Officer Training course. Armed Traffic Cars additionally require firearms education.

### Conditional requirements

Some verified airfield incidents mark Traffic Cars as `only_when_available`. This is a conditional field, not a guaranteed response and not merely a strategic recommendation.

Preserve the condition when creating dispatch plans. Do not permanently allocate conditional units as though every relevant mission guarantees them.

### Geographic posture

- Position Traffic Cars along high-demand corridors rather than only at the largest depot.
- Preserve ordinary patrol cover when roads units deploy.
- Train replacement personnel before relying on one dual-trained Armed Traffic Car.
- Include travel time to airfields, motorways and cross-region incidents.

## Dog-support planning

The DSU and Multiple Dog Carrier use the verified five-day Dog handling course.

### DSU versus Multiple Dog Carrier

- DSU is a deployable dog-support resource on verified missions.
- Multiple Dog Carrier transports up to four dogs at maximum crew.
- A carrier's capacity does not prove that every mission accepts it as a substitute for several DSUs.
- Keep the mission requirement and the vehicle transport contract separate until dispatch substitution is directly reproduced.

### Recommended dog reserve

For each region where dog requirements recur:

1. maintain one dispatchable dog-support route;
2. train replacement handlers;
3. duplicate by travel-time zone when one unit becomes a geographic bottleneck;
4. test overlap with public-order, firearms and railway demand.

## Police air support

Police Helicopter has verified aerial-search and air-support capabilities and requires the seven-day Police aviation course.

Recommended planning:

- place aircraft by regional travel advantage;
- maintain trained replacement personnel before treating one helicopter as resilient;
- preserve ground Police capacity because air support does not replace scene, custody or personnel requirements;
- respect mission-specific alternatives such as Police Helicopter **or** Drone rather than inventing universal substitution.

## Eight expansion gates

| Gate | Question | Recommended pass condition |
|---|---|---|
| Routine reserve | Can ordinary calls dispatch after the proposed specialist leaves? | Useful Police Car cover remains in each response cluster |
| Personnel | Are the correct educated staff available and assigned? | Vehicle seats and qualified people are both available |
| Supervision | Can Sergeant and Inspector requirements be fulfilled independently? | Required command roles are not borrowed twice |
| Custody | Can likely prisoner demand be moved without exhausting scene response? | Verified custody capacity and return time are included |
| Geography | Will the resource reach its intended mission family? | Placement improves actual route coverage |
| Cross-service | Are Ambulance, HART, Fire, Railway or SAR dependencies ready? | The whole response chain can operate |
| Concurrency | Can two reference incidents run without reusing the same unit or cohort? | No hidden personnel, custody or specialist conflict remains |
| Recovery | Can patrol and specialist reserve be restored afterwards? | Returning, transporting and repositioning resources are counted |

These are strategy templates, not MissionChief requirements.

### Foundation network

| Capability | Recommended position |
|---|---|

| Protected patrol reserve | At least 1 useful response route after a normal dispatch |
| Custody | Use existing verified transport until prisoner demand demonstrates a dedicated Detention Van need |
| Armed / roads / dog | Alliance-supported initially, then one local trained route when repeated delays appear |
| Public order | Do not activate until officer, supervisor and transport readiness is complete |
| Air support | Add only when geography and trained staffing justify the regional capability |

| Roads | One trained Traffic Car route; duplicate by corridor when travel time proves the need |

| Capability | Recommended position |
|---|---|

| Armed, roads and dogs | Independently dispatchable groups by major travel-time zone |

## Placement doctrine

### Dense urban network

- Place Police Cars around mission and custody geography.
- Distribute public-order transport and supervisors rather than centralising every cohort.
- Keep armed, roads and dog resources close enough to support several districts.
- Include congestion and prisoner transport time in availability.

### Rural or dispersed network

- Keep routine patrol local because neighbouring cover may be slow.
- Duplicate armed and dog capability by travel-time region before raw fleet size alone justifies it.
- Use air support where it provides measurable regional value.
- Treat alliance support as contingency.

### Major-event network

- Pre-position public-order personnel, mounted units, dogs, custody and medical support.
- Maintain a separate routine-patrol floor.
- Test prisoner and patient maxima alongside the vehicle response.
- Restore local cover and specialist geography before declaring the network ready again.

## Recovery-to-readiness

After a major Police incident:

1. record units still on scene, transporting prisoners or returning;
2. restore routine Police Car cover first;
3. confirm specialist vehicles and educated crews are together again;
4. return Sergeant, Inspector and public-order cohorts to useful regions;
5. restore custody, dog, air and roads capability;
6. review cross-service missions still consuming resources;
7. rerun the selected concurrency scenario;
8. correct recurring bottlenecks before activating more extensions.

Mission completion is not proof that routine patrol, custody and specialist reserve are restored.

## Common failures

| Failure | Operational symptom | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Buying specialists before patrol capacity | Routine missions stall while rare vehicles remain parked | Restore Police Car reserve first |
| Counting vehicle seats as trained personnel | Carriers exist but public-order or armed requirements remain open | Audit educated cohorts separately |
| Activating public order too early | Large personnel and prisoner missions appear before readiness | Complete officers, supervisors, transport and custody first |
| Ignoring custody cycle time | Scene response is adequate but Police vehicles remain absent transporting prisoners | Add dedicated capacity or reposition stations |
| Reusing one cohort across several vehicles | Multiple specialists appear available but only one can crew | Assign explicit cohort ownership |
| Treating conditional Traffic Cars as guaranteed | Roads units are permanently over-allocated | Preserve the `only_when_available` condition |
| Expanding acronyms without evidence | EIU is given an unsupported full name | Retain the official abbreviation |
| Centralising every specialist | One incident removes regional armed, dog or public-order capability | Duplicate by travel-time zone |
| Planning Police alone | Ambulance, HART, Fire, Railway or SAR rows remain open | Audit the full mission contract |
| Treating mission completion as restored readiness | Custody and returning units leave patrol depleted | Run the recovery sequence |
